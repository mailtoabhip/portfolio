Flagship case studyDelhivery · 2026 · Live5 min read

Taking fleet services off the phone and into the app

DelFin is Delhivery's app for truck owners and drivers. When I joined, almost everything it was meant to do still happened over phone calls and paper. This is the story of turning it into a product people can use on their own, and what the live data says about it.

At a glance · the 30-second version
The problem

Fleet owners bought FASTags and GPS trackers through sales calls and paper KYC, then waited with no status. Every order needed a person on our side.

What I did

Designed DelFin end to end: onboarding, FASTag purchase and tracking, GPS, wallet, GST invoices and refunds, on the Tarmac Design System.

The outcome

80% of FASTag orders now reach an active tag inside the app, in about 6 hours, with no sales call. 98% of new users finish setup.

The story

A truck stuck at a toll is a business problem

Picture a fleet owner with a handful of trucks on the highway. Every truck needs a FASTag to pass a toll plaza. If a tag is inactive, blacklisted or low on balance, the truck stops, the driver calls the owner, and the owner calls whoever sold them the tag.

When I joined Delhivery, that was DelFin's reality. Buying a tag meant a call with sales, KYC documents sent separately, and then waiting with no idea where the order was. Problems surfaced at the toll, not before it.

For the business, the same picture looked like this: every single order needed a salesperson or a support agent. Growth meant hiring. My brief was to make DelFin something owners and drivers could use on their own.

Who it's for

Two users sharing one truck

Owners and drivers share the same vehicles but need very different things, so the app has to know who it's talking to from the first screen.

Fleet owner

Pays and manages

Owns one or more trucks. Buys tags and trackers, needs GST invoices to claim tax credit, and wants to see every vehicle at once.

Driver

Drives and recharges

Drives someone else's truck. Needs the tag to work at the toll and an easy way to recharge, without seeing the owner's money or documents.

What I designed

Five areas, one consistent set of patterns

I designed DelFin end to end, working with the PM and engineers on scope and trade-offs. Rather than five separate features, I treated it as a few reusable patterns: buy, verify, pay, track, and fix. Once those worked for FASTag, every other service could start from them.

Onboarding

Get to the right first screen

Benefit-led tutorial, phone OTP and an owner-or-driver split. Live.

FASTag

Buy and track a tag

RC auto-fill, KYC with fallbacks, itemised payment, live activation status. Live.

Money

GST, wallet and refunds

GSTIN at payment, bulk invoices, self-serve refunds. Designed.

GPS

Track the fleet

Device purchase, installation booking and a running/idle/stopped dashboard. Live.

Home & consent

One place for what needs action

Action-led home, role-based profile and clear data-sharing consent with AXLE. Designed.

From a sales call to an active FASTag in about 6 hours

Buying a FASTag used to take a phone call, paper KYC and a lot of waiting. I redesigned the whole journey, from choosing a bank to the tag working on the truck. The surprise: the biggest wins came after the payment screen, not before it.

The problem

Owners bought tags over the phone, sent KYC on paper, paid separately and then had no idea where their order was.

What I did

Designed the in-app purchase (RC auto-fill, KYC with fallbacks, itemised payment) and the post-purchase journey (handover, activation, action-needed alerts).

The outcome

The story

"Where is my tag?"

If you asked a fleet owner what buying a FASTag from us felt like, the honest answer was: a phone call, then paperwork, then silence.

They'd call sales, share their RC and KYC documents separately, pay, and wait. Nothing told them whether the tag had been issued, shipped or activated. Often the first sign of a problem was a truck stuck at a toll plaza.

So I didn't start with the checkout. I started by asking what an owner actually needs to stop worrying: knowing the tag is coming, knowing it's working, and hearing about problems before the toll does.

Before & after

What changed for the owner

Before
  • Owner calls sales to place an order
  • KYC documents shared on paper or chat
  • Payment and GST handled separately
  • No status after the order
  • Tag problems found at the toll
After
  • Order inside the app in a few steps
  • Vehicle details fetched from the RC
  • KYC, payment and GST in one flow
  • Live status from payment to activation
  • Problems flagged on the home screen first
Constraints

Why this was harder than a checkout

A FASTag isn't an ordinary product. A bank issues it, it's tied to one vehicle's registration, and someone physically sticks it on a truck. Each of those shaped the design.

Two banks

Different KYC rules

Tags are issued by partner banks (IDFC First and Axis), each with its own KYC requirements.

Vehicle data

Trust the RC, not typing

Vehicle class, owner and registration must match the RC exactly, or the bank rejects the tag.

Physical step

A tag changes hands

Every order ends with a physical handover and a tag stuck on a truck, which the app has to track.

Real fleets

Messy edge cases

Owners bring tags bought elsewhere, have several customer IDs and manage many vehicles at once.

Key decisions

Five decisions that shaped the flow

1. Let the RC fill in the form

The issueOwners typed vehicle details by hand, and small mistakes caused bank rejections.
What I didUpload the RC once; the app fetches the vehicle details and asks the owner to confirm, not type.
ResultFewer fields to type, and data the bank accepts first time.

2. Give KYC a fallback, not a dead end

The issueIf one KYC method failed, the only option was calling sales.
What I didPick the bank first, then a checklist of KYC methods (OTP, Aadhaar, biometric, PAN), with "Alternative KYC methods" one tap away.
ResultKYC no longer ends in a call to sales when one method fails.

3. Show every rupee before payment

The issueOwners didn't trust a total they couldn't break down.
What I didAn itemised summary: tag, vehicle, minimum balance, platform fee with GST, issuance fee and grand total, with GSTIN on the same screen.
Result84% of created orders are paid.

4. Treat activation as part of the purchase

The issueAfter paying, owners couldn't tell if their tag was coming or working.
What I didTrack the order through handover and activation with live status; the delivery agent scans the tag barcode in the app.
Result95% of paid orders end with an active tag, in about 6 hours.

5. Surface problems before the toll

The issueOwners found out about low balance or blacklisting only when a truck was stopped.
What I did"Action needed" cards on the home screen, tags split into active and inactive, and problem tags pinned to the top.
Result68% of users on the home screen open their FASTags.

New users didn't know what DelFin was for, and owners and drivers got the same setup. I rebuilt onboarding around one early question. Then the data seemed to say it was failing, and the most useful thing I did was not redesign it.

The story

Same app, two very different people

The owner pays for everything and needs GST invoices. The driver just needs the tag to work and should never see the owner's money or documents. Yet both opened DelFin and got exactly the same setup, with no explanation of what the app was even for.

So the first screen a new user sees now says what DelFin does for them, and the first real question it asks is: are you the owner, or the driver?

Key decisions

Four decisions

1. Lead with benefits, not features

The issueNew users didn't know what DelFin offered.
What I didA short tutorial with three concrete benefits: instant FASTag activation, a low minimum balance and timely low-balance alerts.
ResultUsers start login knowing what they get.

2. Phone OTP, with consent built in

The issueSign-up asked for too much too early.
What I didPhone number and OTP only, with the privacy policy and terms accepted on the same screen.
Result89% of users who start login finish it.

3. Ask "owner or driver?" first

The issueOne setup for two very different users.
What I didTwo plain-language choices: "I own one or more vehicles" and "I drive someone else's vehicle". Drivers add the owner's details instead of their own documents.
Result98.3% answer it; only 1.7% abandon this step.

4. Make data sharing a clear choice

The issueProfiles were incomplete and data syncing with AXLE was opaque.
What I didAn action-led home, role-based profile completion and granular, revocable consent for sharing data with AXLE.
ResultUsers can see and control what is shared.
Reading the data

The day the data said we'd failed

A week after launch, the onboarding funnel showed 37% of users leaving between login and role selection. On a dashboard, that looks like the biggest leak in the app, and the obvious next step was to redesign role selection.

Before doing that, I put it next to a second funnel from the same week. 1,422 users reached the home screen, but only 944 ever saw role selection. Returning users skip setup and go straight home. Most of that 37% never needed the screen at all.

Reflection

What I learned

Check a number before you act on it. A second funnel saved a redesign of a screen that was working.

One good question beats a long form. Splitting by role early made every later screen shorter.

DelFin · Deep dive 3Fintech · Designed3 min read

Self-serve GST invoices and refunds for fleet owners

Fleet owners were quietly overpaying 18% on our fees because they couldn't add a GSTIN, and money in their wallet could only come back through a support ticket. I designed both flows so owners handle tax and refunds themselves.

At a glance · the 30-second version
The problem

No GSTIN support, so no input tax credit. Wallet money stuck without a self-serve refund.

What I did

GSTIN at payment with PAN auto-fetch and live validation, bulk GST invoices, and self-serve wallet-to-bank refunds with a clear partial-refund case.

The outcome

Owners can claim 18% input tax credit and raise a refund in under 2 minutes. Wallet top-ups finish 72% of the time once started.

The story

Money that was stuck, and credit left on the table

For a business, a GST invoice isn't paperwork. It's 18% of the fee coming back as input tax credit. Without GSTIN support in the app, every owner paying us was overpaying, and their accountant was emailing support for invoices one by one.

The wallet had the same shape of problem. Owners topped up to pay for tags and trackers, but the only way to get unused money back was to raise a ticket and wait. Nothing erodes trust in a fintech product faster than money you can't get out.

Before
  • No GSTIN in the app, so no tax credit
  • Invoices requested from support, one by one
  • Wallet money stuck without a withdrawal option
  • Refund status unknown
After
  • GSTIN at payment, auto-fetched from PAN
  • Bulk GST invoices by date range
  • Refund to bank from wallet history
  • Refund amount capped and tracked live
Key decisions

Three decisions

1. Ask for GSTIN where it pays off

The issueA GSTIN field buried in settings would never be found.
What I didGSTIN on the payment summary, auto-fetched from PAN, validated live and saved to the profile for next time.
ResultOwners claim 18% input tax credit without contacting support.

2. Invoices in bulk, not one at a time

The issueAccountants need a month of invoices, not one receipt.
What I didDownload GST invoices for any date range from Order History.
ResultTax filing no longer depends on a support queue.

3. Design the unhappy path first

The issueWallet money could only come back through a ticket, and edge cases were where trust broke.
What I didStart a refund from wallet history, pick a reason and see the refunded amount. When the balance is below the minimum, the app explains the partial refund instead of failing.
ResultA refund takes under 2 minutes to raise.
How we'll measure it

The numbers this has to move

Support

Fewer tickets

GST invoice and refund tickets per 1,000 orders, and refund follow-up queries.

Adoption

Self-serve rate

Share of invoices and refunds done in the app, GSTIN adoption, and drop-off at the GST step.

Quality

Fewer errors

Invalid or expired GSTINs on invoices, and failed refunds returned to the wallet.

Pennsylvania Department of Health · Self-service kiosk · UX design

Designing a kiosk that people can actually finish using.

A government certificate request shouldn't require expert knowledge of government systems. This case study follows the audit, the redesign decisions, the actual interface and the results reported by the project.

UX designResearch & heuristicsPublic services

01 / CONTEXT

Why this mattered

A birth certificate may be one document, but obtaining one can be a high-pressure, unfamiliar task.

The Pennsylvania Department of Health kiosk was intended to let residents complete requests independently. The existing journey contained too much ambiguity: hard-to-follow instructions, repeated information, inconsistent interaction patterns, and limited opportunities to verify details before submission.

34%

of requests were reported as abandoned in the original project case study.

02 / EVIDENCE

Over 100 issues. Four themes.

We evaluated the original kiosk using Nielsen's ten usability heuristics and logged more than 100 issues with severity ratings. The findings clustered into guidance, efficiency, confidence and consistency.

GUIDANCE

Unclear instructions and error messages made it difficult to recover.

EFFICIENCY

Repeated entry and cumbersome verification increased effort.

CONFIDENCE

No simple certificate preview before committing to the request.

CONSISTENCY

Actions and interface conventions changed between screens.

Who was the kiosk for?

A public kiosk has to work for people with very different abilities and experience levels. We considered a typical resident, an older user, someone with limited upper-limb dexterity, and a Spanish-speaking resident.

This mattered to decisions about reading and touch targets, predictable navigation, language choice, and how much information to ask at once.

03 / APPROACH

A journey people could follow.

Rather than applying a visual refresh to each isolated screen, the redesigned experience made the next action clear throughout the request.

01Identify

Choose language, type of request, provide contact and identity information.

02Review

Check the certificate information, return to edit errors, and acknowledge.

03Payment

Proceed to copies, payment and confirmation in the wider flow.

The original screenshots below visibly cover identification, certificate preview and acknowledgement. Payment is part of the planned journey, but its individual screens are not present in these two available high-fidelity boards.

05 / REPORTED OUTCOME

Less friction, more completed requests.

The earlier project write-up reports measurable changes in request time, abandonment, errors and user satisfaction.

25 → 4–5 minReported request completion time
34% → 15%Reported abandonment
22% → 8%Reported user-error rate
3.5 → 4.7Reported satisfaction out of 5

Source: outcomes stated in the earlier portfolio case study. The underlying analytics and testing datasets were not provided for independent verification; these should be treated as reported project results.

06 / REFLECTION

Less interpretation. More confidence.

What worked here wasn't decoration. It was making the service legible.

A user shouldn't need to memorise what each step means, ask a member of staff to decode an error, or submit a certificate without seeing the details. A public service becomes more usable when the system explains what is happening and what comes next.

Auditing two TVS websites, and getting the fixes prioritised

Finding usability problems is the easy part. The hard part is getting a busy team to fix them next to feature work. This is how we audited two TVS websites and turned the findings into a roadmap the client agreed to.

At a glance · the 30-second version
The problem

The TVS iQube and three-wheeler websites had inconsistent navigation, cluttered content and low contrast, but fixes kept losing to feature work.

What I did

Heuristic evaluations of both sites against Nielsen's 10 principles, severity ratings, findings presentations and a prioritisation workshop with the client.

The outcome

Severity-rated reports, critical issues shortlisted into sprints, and work started on standard buttons, menus and navigation.

  • ScopeTVS iQube and TVS 3W websites
  • CompanyExtentia, a Merkle company

The story

A list of problems is not a plan

Every team knows its website has issues. What it rarely has is a clear, ranked list of which ones hurt users most, and a reason to fix them before the next feature.

So we treated this as two jobs: find the issues rigorously, then make it easy for the client to decide what to do about them.

The process

From audit to roadmap

  1. AuditHeuristic evaluation of both sites against Nielsen's 10 usability principles.
  2. RateEvery issue categorised by heuristic and rated by severity.
  3. PresentFindings shared with each site's team.
  4. PrioritiseA workshop with the client and internal teams to agree a roadmap.
What we found

Key issues on each site

Three-wheelers

TVS 3W

  • Hamburger menu in different places on different pages
  • Too many colours and too much text
  • Buttons and navigation without clear states
  • Few ways to undo or exit key actions
Electric scooter

TVS iQube

  • Features and specifications buried in long text
  • Long scrolls with an unclear section hierarchy
  • Inconsistent button and tab styles
  • Low contrast, especially on mobile
